## 1. The one-paragraph version

Millions of people manage their own money and test their own trading ideas,
and almost every one of them does it in a way a professional would recognise
immediately as broken: they tune a strategy until it looks good on the past,
mistake that for evidence, and put real money behind it. The fix is well
established and completely unavailable to them — hold back data the strategy
has never seen, judge it only on that, and throw it away if it fails. Strategy
Lab AI is the first product that makes that discipline the default rather than
an option, generating and testing up to a million strategy variations against
ten years of real market data and then refusing to call any of them
"qualified" unless they clear eleven fixed statistical gates. Around that sit
three more products sharing the same data and the same honesty: AI portfolio
research, full equity research reports, and around-the-clock monitoring of the
strategies that survived. It is built, it is live, and it is run by one person
at a cost structure that would have required a funded team a few years ago.
**I am raising $250,000 to license the market data, settle one regulatory
question, and work on this full time.**

## 3. Why now

Four things became true at once, and they had to.

**1. AI made the explanation layer economically possible.** The single most
valuable thing this product does is tell a user *why* — why a strategy failed,
why a signal fired, what a company's numbers actually mean. Two years ago
producing that at per-user scale was cost-prohibitive. Today it is cents per
call, and it is metered.

**2. Retail participation structurally reset.** The number of people managing
their own money has not returned to pre-2020 levels. The audience exists now
in a way it did not a decade ago.

**3. The audience became able to understand the pitch.** "Overfitting" and
"out-of-sample" have moved out of academic papers and into retail trading
discourse. Five years ago the differentiator would have needed explaining
before it could be sold. Now people arrive already looking for it.

**4. Infrastructure costs collapsed.** Serverless hosting, managed Postgres
and pay-per-token AI mean one person can build and operate a platform that
would have required a funded team and a data centre contract in 2018. The
whole thing currently runs at effectively infrastructure-only cost.

**The window.** The audience is ready, the technology is cheap, and the
incumbents have not moved — because moving means telling their existing users
that most of their saved strategies were never really validated. That is a
product decision with an immediate churn cost for them, and no cost at all for
us. That asymmetry is temporary, and it is the reason to move now.

---

## 4. The problem

Not a lack of data. An abundance of data and a total absence of judgement
between "I have an idea" and "I am risking money."

**Retail backtesting is quietly, systematically broken.** When someone tunes a
strategy until it looks good on their whole price history, they have not
discovered anything — they have memorised the past. The professional defence
is out-of-sample validation. Almost no retail tool enforces it. Many do not
offer it. The ones that offer it make it optional, which in practice means off,
because turning it on makes your results look worse.

**The serious tools are built for programmers.** The genuinely powerful
platforms expect Python, a data API and an environment you manage yourself.
The learning curve is months, and it filters out exactly the people who most
need protecting from their own conclusions.

**Nothing explains why.** A chart shows a buy arrow. It does not say *this
fired because RSI(14) dropped below 30 while price was still above its
50-period moving average — a pullback inside an uptrend.* Without the why,
there is no learning, so the mistake repeats.

**And the information environment is actively hostile.** The loudest voices
sell certainty: signal groups, screenshot P&Ls, courses. They are optimised
for conversion, not for being right. An honest tool that sometimes says "your
idea failed, here is exactly which test it failed and by how much" competes
against an entire market that never says that.

**What it costs people:** real money, repeatedly, without ever diagnosing why
— because without validation there is no feedback loop at all.

---

## 5. The insight

**The product is not the backtest. The product is the refusal.**

Every competitor optimises. Optimising is easy, and it is what users think
they want. We optimise and then systematically try to break the result, and we
report the breakage as prominently as the success.

This looks like a feature. It is actually a positioning decision with three
consequences that compound:

**It makes our results look worse — which is the signal.** A serious user who
sees a platform reject 94% of what it generated learns more about that
platform in five seconds than any marketing copy could tell them. The rejection
rate *is* the credibility.

**It makes the failures valuable.** "Out-of-sample Sharpe is 0.41, below the
required 0.75" is a genuinely useful sentence. The user learns something from
a strategy that did not work — which means the product delivers value even
when it delivers bad news. That is a rare property, and it is why retention
does not depend on anyone making money.

**It is structurally hard for an incumbent to copy.** Adopting enforced
out-of-sample validation means telling your existing users that most of what
they saved was never validated. We have no legacy users to disappoint.

# Part II — The company

## 6. The product

Four products, one data layer, one rule: **never state a number we did not
compute from real data, and never imply a trade is a good idea.**

### 6.1 Research — the engine

- **Twelve strategy families**, each a real named technique: Trend Following,
  Mean Reversion, Breakout, Momentum, Volume Confirmation, RSI Regime, Moving
  Average Cross, MACD Trend, Bollinger Band Reversion, Donchian Breakout, VWAP
  Pullback, ATR Trend.
- **Up to 1,000,000 parameter variations per run**, against up to **ten years**
  of real data at daily, hourly, 15-minute and live resolutions.
- **Mandatory walk-forward validation** — every run splits into an in-sample
  portion the strategy is fitted on and an out-of-sample portion it has never
  seen, then evaluates across multiple independent validation windows.
- **Eleven fixed qualification gates**, in one place in the code, not
  adjustable by the user:

  | Gate | Requirement |
  |---|---|
  | Data integrity | All mechanical sanity checks pass |
  | Data source | Real market data only — synthetic can never qualify |
  | Total trades | ≥ 50 |
  | Out-of-sample trades | ≥ 20 |
  | OOS expectancy | > $0 per trade |
  | OOS profit factor | ≥ 1.15 |
  | OOS Sharpe ratio | ≥ 0.75 |
  | OOS return | > 0% |
  | OOS max drawdown | ≤ 10% |
  | Window consistency | ≥ 60% of validation windows profitable |
  | Profit concentration | No single window > 60% of total profit |

- **A plain-English rejection reason for every failure**, naming the test, the
  value achieved and the value required.
- **$100,000 simulated research capital.** Not real money. No order is ever
  placed anywhere, by any code path.
- **Successful Strategy Log** and **full Run History** — accumulated personal
  research that makes leaving expensive.

### 6.2 Portfolios and A-TAMP

- Brokerage-accurate position tracking: buying more of a held symbol merges
  into one position at a share-weighted average cost; selling writes a
  realised trade.
- Return decomposed into **total, unrealised, realised and today's** — at the
  account, portfolio and individual-holding level. Selling a winner never
  erases it from your track record.
- **Return over time**, from hourly server-side snapshots that accrue whether
  or not anyone has the site open, backfilled from daily closes for any period
  before tracking began.
- **A-TAMP** — Automated Trading Analysis & Monitoring Platform. Takes a
  plain-language theme, pulls real live and historical prices across a curated
  universe of **76 liquid US tickers**, scores every candidate on real momentum
  and volatility, and proposes an example allocation with a factual, data-cited
  rationale per position. The AI may only choose from the preset universe;
  anything it invents is filtered out server-side and weights are
  re-normalised by us. Without an AI key it falls back to a transparent
  rules-based screen **and says so**. The user reviews, then applies or
  discards. Nothing is automatic.
- A **complete change log** per portfolio — every edit, every AI rebalance with
  before-and-after state and per-symbol reasoning.

### 6.3 Company Reports

A full equity research report on any public company from real data: business
overview, competitor benchmarking on growth and margins, financial health
across income, balance sheet and cash flow, management and ownership including
insider transactions, analyst coverage and targets, catalysts, technicals,
shareholder returns, and news — with an AI-written synthesis on top covering
business explanation, competitive analysis, brand strength, SWOT and a final
assessment.

The model is given only the real bundle and is required to write exactly "Not
publicly reported" or "Data unavailable" where something is missing. It cannot
invent a competitor, an executive, a figure or an event. Reports are saved,
searchable and favouritable — and they are the single most shareable artefact
the product produces, which is why one free report is the Free-tier taster.

### 6.4 Trade Signals

Favourite a qualified strategy and it is checked against live prices — in the
browser while the page is open, and by a server-side job **every five minutes
regardless of whether anyone is logged in**. When a rule fires, a notification
is created with the strategy, action, price and a plain-English reason. The
user confirms or dismisses; confirming opens or closes a tracked paper
position. **No order is ever sent anywhere.** If the background job goes
stale, the page says so to the affected user — a monitoring product that
silently stops monitoring is worse than none.

## 8. Technology

A Next.js 16 application on Vercel over Supabase Postgres. Three properties
matter to an investor:

**1. The expensive compute runs on the user's machine.** The backtesting
engine executes in the browser. A user running a million-variation search
costs us nothing. This decouples our most visible cost driver from usage
entirely, and it is the main reason an 80%+ gross margin is credible rather
than aspirational.

**2. The product works while the user is asleep.** Signal checks every five
minutes and portfolio snapshots every hour run server-side on a schedule,
authenticated by a constant-time shared-secret comparison sent as a header.
This is what makes it a subscription rather than a tool you open occasionally.

**3. Every rule lives in exactly one place.** Qualification thresholds: one
module. Plan limits: one database table, changeable without a deployment.
Legal copy: one set of versioned markdown files. This is what makes it
possible for one person to operate the whole system without the marketing page
and the enforcement layer drifting apart — the classic failure mode for a
solo-built SaaS, and the reason this one does not have it.

**Security.** Row-level security on every user table, bound to the
authenticated user and — for portfolio-scoped tables — to the owning portfolio.
Sessions validated server-side against the auth provider on every protected
request, never decoded from a cookie. No secret reachable from the browser.
Distributed rate limiting per IP and per account. Full security header set:
content security policy, HSTS with preload, frame denial, referrer and
permissions policy. A written information security program with an annual
review on the calendar.

### How the AI is kept honest

This is a financial product with a language model inside it, which is exactly
the combination regulators and users are right to be suspicious of. The
constraints are architectural, not policy statements:

- **The model never sees a number it did not receive from us.** Both
  integrations are given a bundle of real, computed data and instructed to
  write only from it. Where something is missing, the model must write exactly
  "Not publicly reported" or "Data unavailable" — it is not permitted to
  estimate.
- **The model cannot invent a security.** A-TAMP may only select from a preset
  universe of real tickers. Anything it returns outside that list is discarded
  server-side, and the weights it proposes are re-normalised by us rather than
  trusted.
- **The model cannot give advice.** It is explicitly instructed never to
  promise or predict a return and never to phrase anything as what a person
  should do. Output is framed as an example throughout.
- **The model is never in the loop on an action.** Nothing it produces is
  applied automatically. Every proposal is reviewed and accepted or discarded
  by the user.
- **It degrades honestly.** If the AI is unavailable, both features fall back
  to deterministic, rules-based output — and the output says that is what
  happened, rather than quietly presenting a template as analysis.
- **Its cost is bounded.** Metered per plan in the database, capped per
  account, and capped again deployment-wide.

**Security review is a routine, not an event.** The platform has been through
multiple structured review passes, each recorded in the repository with the
findings and the fixes. The most recent produced, among others: an
open-redirect fix on the login flow, a wildcard-injection fix in the username
lookup, authentication added to the market-data proxy, a deployment-wide
ceiling on AI spend, and database-level hardening of row-level security
defaults. I am telling you this not because it is unusual to have findings —
every system has them — but because the discipline of looking, recording and
fixing is the thing that is unusual at this stage.

## 9. Why this is hard to copy

Honestly: the features are not defensible. A well-funded incumbent could build
the qualification gate in a quarter. Four things are harder.

**1. The posture has a switching cost for them and none for us.** Enforcing
out-of-sample validation means telling your existing users that most of their
saved strategies were never validated. We have no legacy users to disappoint.

**2. The explanation layer is deceptively hard.** Getting a language model to
explain a rejection accurately, in plain English, without ever inventing a
number, without ever drifting into advice, and while degrading gracefully when
the model is unavailable — that is the part that takes real iteration, and it
is invisible until you try to build it.

**3. The compliance surface is a genuine barrier.** A competitor copying our
features inherits our regulatory exposure without our documentation, our
versioned consent trail, or our architectural guarantees that no trade is ever
placed. In this category that is not paperwork; it is the cost of entry.

**4. Accumulated personal history.** Strategy logs, run history, saved
reports, portfolio change logs. A user with a year of research on the platform
does not casually move.

## 10. Market

I am going to be careful here, because inflated market sizing is the fastest
way to lose credibility with someone who does this professionally.

**Who buys.** The serious self-directed retail investor — manages their own
money, has been doing it at least a year, has already lost money on an idea
that "looked good" and knows it, typically 25–45, comfortable with software
but not a programmer. Secondary: the aspiring quant, using us as the on-ramp
before Python — high engagement, price-sensitive, the Free-to-Pro cohort.
Tertiary: small advisory practices and investment clubs — the Elite tier and
the first real enterprise conversation.

**Who does not.** Day traders wanting signals to copy. Anyone wanting
guaranteed returns. Institutions with a Bloomberg terminal and a quant team.
We will lose all three and should.

**Sizing, honestly.** Tens of millions of US adults hold a self-directed
brokerage account — that is the outer boundary and it is not our market, since
most of them buy index funds and never think about a strategy. The realistic
addressable market is the subset who actively research and test their own
ideas and already pay for tools; the honest proxy is the paying subscriber
base of existing charting and backtesting platforms, collectively in the low
millions of paid seats globally at $15–60 per month.

**What I am actually underwriting.** 10,000 paying subscribers at a blended
$35/month is roughly **$4.2M ARR**. That is a genuine success and it requires
capturing a trivial share of anything. **Every projection in this memorandum
is built up from conversion and retention assumptions, not down from a market
size** — precisely because I do not want the investment case to rest on a
number I cannot defend under questioning.

# Part III — The business

## 12. Business model

Subscription SaaS. Three tiers. Every expensive operation metered, with the
meters in a database table rather than in code, so pricing is tunable without
a deployment.

| | **Free** | **Pro** | **Elite** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Price** | $0 | **$29/mo · $290/yr** | **$99/mo · $990/yr** |
| Portfolios | 1 | 10 | 100 |
| Watchlist symbols | 3 | 25 | 150 |
| Live-monitored strategies | 2 | 25 | 150 |
| AI company reports / month | 1 | 30 | 200 |
| AI portfolio runs / month | 0 | 30 | 200 |
| Max variations per run | 10,000 | 1,000,000 | 1,000,000 |

**The three decisions behind that table.**

**Live-monitored strategies is the load-bearing limit.** Every favourited
strategy is re-evaluated every five minutes, forever. It is the only limit
that creates a permanent recurring cost per user. Free at two is deliberate.

**A-TAMP is zero on Free.** It is the clearest "this is the paid product"
moment in the app. Giving it away once teaches people it is free. One free
*company report* is the better taster because it produces a document the user
wants to keep and share — which also makes it our best organic acquisition
surface.

**Elite is capped at 200, never sold as unlimited.** You do not sell unlimited
against a metered upstream API. 200 reports a month at roughly $0.10–0.15 each
is about $25 of AI cost against $99 of revenue — a margin that holds even for
the heaviest possible user.

**One rule that will not change:** nothing a user created is ever deleted,
hidden or locked when they downgrade. A lapsed Pro user with eight portfolios
keeps reading all eight; they simply cannot create a ninth. Limits apply on
create, never on read. Deleting a former customer's data is how you earn a
chargeback and a public complaint, and neither is worth the upsell.

**Future revenue lines, deliberately excluded from the projections** so the
case stands on subscriptions alone: an enterprise/team tier for advisory
practices, API access to the qualification engine sold per call, structured
educational content, and eventually white-label for brokers.

## 30. Glossary

**Backtest** — running a strategy against historical data to see how it would
have performed.
**Out-of-sample** — data the strategy was never optimised on. The only honest
test.
**Overfitting** — tuning a strategy until it fits the past so closely it has
learned noise rather than anything real.
**Walk-forward validation** — repeatedly optimising on one period and testing
on the next, to confirm a result holds across time rather than in one window.
**Sharpe ratio** — return relative to volatility. Higher is better
risk-adjusted performance.
**Profit factor** — gross profit ÷ gross loss. Above 1.0 is profitable; 1.15
is our floor.
**Expectancy** — average profit or loss per trade.
**Maximum drawdown** — the largest peak-to-trough decline in account value.
**Realised / unrealised return** — gain already locked in by selling / paper
gain on positions still held.
**MRR / ARR** — monthly / annual recurring revenue.
**CAC / LTV** — customer acquisition cost / lifetime value.
**Churn** — the rate at which subscribers cancel.
